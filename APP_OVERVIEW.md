# University Simulator: what it does and what it has

University Simulator is a game that lets South African students rehearse university life
before it happens. A student sets up a character that matches their real situation, then
plays short chapters from matric to graduation, making the choices a real student faces
about money, marks and wellbeing. Each choice shows what it leads to and which campus
service helps. Every chapter ends with a personal forecast and a survival plan to keep.

It runs in a phone browser, installs like an app, and works offline after the first load.
There is no account and nothing personal leaves the phone.

## The problem it answers

> How might technology help young South Africans find suitable study pathways, compare
> funding options, complete applications and remain supported from acceptance through
> graduation?

| Part of the problem | Where the app answers it |
|---|---|
| Find suitable study pathways | *Before You Apply* chapter: choosing a course |
| Compare funding options | *Before You Apply* (NSFAS and bursaries), and the funding choice in character setup |
| Complete applications | *Before You Apply*: application fees, forms, deadlines and scams |
| Remain supported from acceptance through graduation | The other six chapters, Campus services, the Semester Forecast and the survival plan |

The depth of the app is in the last row. Of the listed focus areas it covers applications
and NSFAS guidance, cost planning, student wellness and academic persistence. For
connectivity, it is built to work offline and on little data.

## How a student uses it

1. **Create a character.** Eleven short questions: university, campus, where they are in
   the journey (still deciding, accepted, already a student), course, housing, funding,
   monthly money, where home is, whether they have a laptop, whether family depends on
   them, and age or role.
2. **Play a chapter, week by week.** Each week brings a situation and two or three choices.
   Three meters move: money, marks and wellbeing. An answer can be changed straight after
   it is made.
3. **Discover the support.** Choosing to get help opens a card for the campus service: what
   it offers, where it is on the campus map, its hours and how to reach it.
4. **Get a forecast and a plan.** After every chapter the app predicts the student's
   riskiest weeks and builds a survival plan they can save or share.

## The journey

Seven chapters, grouped on the home screen into four stages. Any chapter can be played in
any order, so a matric learner and a second-year both have a place to start.

| Stage | Chapter | What it covers | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before You Apply | Before You Apply | Choosing a course, application fees, funding forms, results day | 4 steps, about 5 min |
| First Year | Arrival | First night away, registration surprises, O-Week | 3 steps, about 5 min |
| First Year | First Term | Lecture shock, Societies Day, a late allowance, family requests, test week | 8 weeks, about 10 min |
| First Year | First Exams | Study help, three exams in four days, results, the holidays | 6 weeks, about 6 min |
| First Year | Bounce Back | Warning letters, repeats, doubts about the course | 6 weeks, about 6 min |
| Middle Years | Middle Years | Old fees, moving off campus, choosing a major, part-time work, burnout | 6 weeks, about 6 min |
| Final Year | Finish Line | Final-year projects, job applications, old debts, graduation | 6 weeks, about 6 min |

Behind these are 106 situation cards and 25 one-tap mini questions. A single play-through
shows only some of them, chosen by the student's setup and earlier choices, so two students
get different journeys.

### What happens inside a chapter

- **Choice cards.** A situation, then two or three options. Options the student cannot
  afford are shown but locked.
- **Adaptive cards.** Some cards only appear for some students: a cut bursary for funded
  students, a loan shark for those in debt, and so on.
- **Tests and exams.** Marks are worked out from the student's choices, with a breakdown of
  what helped and what hurt.
- **Exam results and the supplementary exam.** Every student passes or fails their two key
  modules on their real marks. Every student also meets a result code on a third module,
  which admits them to a supplementary exam: the card explains the code, that a supp is a
  second chance, and how to prepare (asking to see the script, past papers). A real fail,
  and a friend's fail, both show what to check: what the module blocks, what it means for
  funding, and when it can be repeated.
- **Friday nights and small spends.** A going-out habit and small temptations that quietly
  drain money, which the forecast later adds up.
- **Surprise expenses.** A stolen phone, a broken laptop, a doctor's visit. An emergency
  buffer softens them.
- **Friend's stories.** Situations that happen to a friend. These are never scored.
- **Wins.** Good moments along the way, so the game is not only about crises.
- **Content notes.** Sensitive cards warn first and can be skipped entirely.

## After each chapter

**Semester Forecast**
- The weeks in the next chapter where this student is most at risk, and why.
- The week money runs short, including what happens if an allowance arrives late.
- Spending leaks per month: nights out, takeaways, clothes, data, subscriptions.
- A short list of actions, worked out on the phone. If the server has an AI key, an
  AI-written version is offered as well.

**Survival plan**
- An editable budget split by percentage.
- An emergency buffer target and how long it takes to save.
- What to do if money arrives late.
- The support services the student met, with how to reach each one.
- Everything the student rehearsed, with the tips.
- It can be saved as an image, or sent to family as a WhatsApp message.

Finishing the last chapter adds a journey summary and a "fair game" reflection, which
names how a harder starting point (no laptop, family to support) changed the odds.

## Campus services

A section on the home screen that makes every support service reachable at any time,
without playing.

- **Named by the need.** 25 entries such as "I'm hungry", "I'm stressed", "I got a result
  code", "My allowance is late", "I don't feel safe".
- **Full service card.** What it offers, the building on the campus map, room, hours, email,
  and any list it points to (accredited accommodation, workshop calendar, result codes).
- **National helplines.** Verified free lines (NSFAS, counselling, Higher Health) are always
  included.
- **Share with a friend.** One tap sends the university name, the service, one line on what
  it offers and a link. It uses the phone's share sheet or WhatsApp. Nothing about the
  sender is included.
- **The link opens the card.** A friend who taps it lands on that service card at the
  sender's university, and is invited to create their own character.

## Words explained

26 terms a first-year may not know (supplementary exam, perusal, NSFAS, bursary, credits,
module, debit order and others) are highlighted in card text. Tapping one opens a short
definition.

## Campus Realities

An optional section on the harder parts of campus life. It has ten topics: xenophobia;
sexual assault and rape; drink spiking and party safety; racism and discrimination; sexual
harassment, including by people in power; abuse in relationships; homophobia and
transphobia; exclusion of students with disabilities; residence initiations and hazing;
and online abuse.

Each topic has five parts: how to recognise it, a short scenario, what to do if it happens
to you, what to do if it happens to a friend, and your rights and where to go. Nothing here
is scored, nothing about what was opened is saved, and a quick-exit button is always on
screen.

## Languages and access

- **Seven languages**: English, isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sepedi, Setswana and Sesotho,
  for menus and story cards. English and isiZulu are written by hand; the other five are
  machine-translated.
- **Offline.** The whole game is stored on the phone on first load. Only the chosen
  university's content is downloaded.
- **Installable** to the home screen.
- **First-time tips** point out each part of the app once, then disappear.
- **Keyboard and screen-reader support** for choices, menus and pop-ups.

## Privacy

- No account, no login, no names.
- Setup answers, choices and progress stay on the phone, and can be erased in one tap.
- The only thing sent to the server is an anonymous count that a chapter was completed,
  plus an optional three-question feedback form.
- Campus Realities is not tracked at all, even on the phone.

## For universities

- **Each university has its own content pack**: name, colours, campuses, campus map,
  services, local costs, result codes and its own cards. Game rules cannot be changed by a
  pack.
- **Two demo universities, both fictional**: Sizwe University (three campuses) and Karoo
  Coast University (one campus, no residences, its own branding).
- **Intake spreadsheets.** A university fills in simple sheets and a tool builds and checks
  its pack.
- **Staff portal.** University staff edit their content on screen. A second person must
  approve before students see it, and every step is recorded.
- **Safety checks before publishing.** A pack is refused if it has an unverified phone
  number, a service in a building that is not on the map, or a story that reaches a dead end.

## What it is built with

- A web app with no framework: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript.
- A small Python server, which is optional. The app plays without it.
- All cards, services and definitions are data files, so content changes need no code.
- 48 automated tests, which play every chapter for every combination of setup answers.

## Honest limits

- Both universities are fictional, so rooms, hours, emails and result codes are examples.
  A real university would supply its own.
- Service descriptions state what a university offers as fact. A real university must
  confirm each one when it signs off its pack.
- The newest isiZulu text (services, definitions, the supplementary exam card) has not yet
  been checked by a native speaker. The five machine-translated languages have not been
  checked at all.
- Campus Realities shows its topic titles in every language, but its guidance is in English
  until a support professional has reviewed the wording.
- Being admitted to a supplementary exam is explained, but writing the supp is not played
  out.
- Marks swing to extremes: mixed choices nearly always pass, and always avoiding help ends
  near zero.
- The app is not professional, financial or medical advice.
